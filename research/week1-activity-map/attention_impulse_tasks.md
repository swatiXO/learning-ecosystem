# Attention / impulse tasks for ages 5–12 (stars_not_clouds, watch_the_pond, wait_for_the_bell, distraction_garden)

Scope note: all numeric parameters below are **literature estimates** tied to the cited source; none have been validated for this app, this tablet, or a Pakistani 5–12 sample. Proprietary instruments (Conners CPT/K-CPT, CPT-II) are cited only for their *published reliability findings*, never as templates for items, wording or scoring (decision log 2026-09-29).

## Q1. What trial counts / task lengths give reliability ≥ .7 for go/no-go and CPT in young children, and how does gamification affect reliability and validity?

### Takeaway
The best-documented open child design (Clark et al. 2023 "fishing game", n=452, ages 3–12) reached internal consistency ω ≈ .83–.85 with ~100 trials per phase over ~8 minutes, but its 5–6-year version dropped to ~.64–.67. Test-retest reliability for CPT/go-no-go scores in children is typically only moderate (≈ .57–.82 raw), so a single short session will rarely give a stable individual score; aggregate across days.

### Cited Findings
- Combined go/no-go + CPT "fishing game" (catch fish, avoid sharks) in 452 children aged 3–12; CFA found two latent factors, labelled response inhibition and sustained attention; limited floor/ceiling effects and developmental sensitivity — [Clark et al. 2023, Psychological Assessment (PMC10041761)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10041761)
- Literature estimate (Clark et al. 2023) of task parameters by age version: v1 (3–4 y) 60 trials/phase, 1500 ms stimulus, 1000 ms ISI; v2 (5–6 y) 80 trials/phase, 750 ms stimulus, 750 ms ISI; v3 (7+ y) 100 trials/phase, 500 ms stimulus, 500 ms ISI; each version ~8 min total. GNG phase = 25% no-go (sharks); CPT phase = 75% sharks (i.e., targets rare) — [Clark et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10041761)
- Literature estimate (Clark et al. 2023): internal consistency McDonald's ω = .83 (response inhibition) and .85 (sustained attention) overall; the 5–6 y version was lower (.67 and .64). No test-retest reported — [Clark et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10041761)
- The authors state that timing parameters had to be adjusted by age to equalize inhibitory demands across age groups — [Clark et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10041761)
- Literature estimate: a commercial CPT for ages 4–7 (7.5 min) had median test-retest r = .57 (n=45, 8–34 day interval) — [Canivez 2017 review of K-CPT 2](https://www.ux1.eiu.edu/~glcanivez/Adobe%20pdf/Publications-Papers/Canivez%20(2017)%20Conners%20K-CPT%202%20Review.pdf) (via search summary; reliability figure only)
- Literature estimate: CPT test-retest in children/adolescents — ICCs .62–.82 for raw scores (omissions, commissions, variability, hit RT) but only .33–.65 for normed T-scores — [PMC2899676](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2899676) (via search summary)
- Literature estimate: an auditory CPT in 105 children aged 5–9 had test-retest .62–.74 across subscales — search summary of [IDEAS/SAGE Open 2013](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/sagope/v3y2013i4p2158244013511828.html)
- Gamification: Pavlovian go/no-go measures showed low test-retest reliability in three independent (adult) samples over 2 weeks to 18 months; a gamified redesign that reduced practice effects raised test-retest to .696–.989 (adult samples) — [Zorowitz, Karni et al. 2025, Computational Psychiatry](https://cpsyjournal.org/articles/127)
- FarmApp: a gamified tablet battery measuring go/no-go response speed, response inhibition, visuospatial STM and LTM, evaluated for feasibility, validity and change over two weeks (detailed numbers not retrieved) — [JMIR Serious Games 2024, e50315](https://games.jmir.org/2024/1/e50315)
- In CPT meta-analysis, measurement error accounted for 32–90% of variance across variables — [Huang-Pollock et al. 2012, J Abnormal Psychology (PMC3664643)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3664643)
- Clark et al.'s game used error feedback (buzzer + broken net on commission errors; bubbling sound + net on correct) — [Clark et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10041761). Note for this app: this conflicts with rule #5 (no fail states); the evidence base therefore partly comes from designs that *do* give error feedback.

### Inferences
- Target ~80–100 trials per construct (go/no-go phase) as a starting estimate for ages 7+; for 5–7 use slower timing and expect lower reliability (≈ .65), so confidence should be capped lower for the youngest band.
- Because test-retest of single sessions is ~.55–.80, the 0–1 confidence should rise with repeated sessions (e.g., day 1 + day 7 repeat, which the week-1 schedule already does for stars_not_clouds per FR-9), not just with trial count within a session.
- Raw scores (not normed T-scores) were more reliable in the CPT-II study — consistent with this app's baseline-referenced (not norm-referenced) design.
- Gamification can improve reliability mainly by reducing practice effects and boosting engagement; it does not automatically preserve validity (see the reskin caveat already in PROJECT.md §12). Replacing error feedback with neutral feedback is a design change whose effect on reliability is untested.

### Gaps
- No retrieved study gives a direct "N no-go trials → reliability" curve for 5–12-year-olds. The classic rule of thumb that commission-error reliability depends mainly on the number of *no-go* trials (not total trials) could not be sourced in this pass.
- FarmApp numeric reliability results could not be extracted (page content failed to load).
- No study found that tested go/no-go reliability with *no* error feedback in children.

## Q2. What are typical commission error rates and RT variability by age 5–12?

### Takeaway
Published values are highly task-dependent; when timing is age-adjusted (as Clark et al. did), commission rates stay ~.28–.33 across age bands, so raw values from one design cannot be transplanted. Sustained-attention performance improves sharply between 6 and 8 years and continues to improve (lower RT variability and omissions) to around age 15.

### Cited Findings
- Literature estimate (Clark et al. 2023, age-adjusted versions, 25% no-go): GNG accuracy .82 (3–4 y), .80 (5–6 y), .73 (7+ y); commission error rate .33 / .28 / .33; d' 1.84 / 1.68 / 1.24. Older children got harder (faster) versions, so these values are not developmental norms on a common task — [Clark et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10041761)
- In 341 school children aged 6–15 on a CPT, hit rate, false-alarm rate and sensitivity related to age quadratically (hit rate and d' rise and level off; false-alarm rate falls and levels off) — [Lin, Hsiao & Chen 1999 via NTU repository](https://scholars.lib.ntu.edu.tw/bitstreams/b009cfc4-4a1a-432c-b6f6-125b9f66bb2d/download) (search summary)
- Major gains in sustained attention between ages 6 and 8, with smaller continued improvement after 8; RT variability and omission errors improved linearly with age (longitudinal) — [J Abnormal Child Psychology 2020, s10802-020-00698-5](https://www.doi.org/10.1007/s10802-020-00698-5) (search summary)
- Sustained-attention performance improves rapidly from ~10 years to mid-teens, reaching adult levels around 15; improvement shows as better accuracy and reduced RT and RT variability — [Dunning et al. 2021 (MRC CBU preprint)](https://www.mrc-cbu.cam.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/www/sites/3/2021/06/Dunning-et-al.-2021.-SART.pdf), citing Lin et al. 1999 and Fortenbaugh et al. 2015
- Boys showed higher commission error rates; children from lower-income households performed lower on several metrics; d' and accuracy summary scores correlated with parent-reported EF and externalizing behaviour — [Clark et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10041761)

### Inferences
- Because scores are compared to the child's own baseline (rule #3), population norms are not required, but age bands must change the *task timing* so 5-year-olds are not at floor and 12-year-olds not at ceiling (Clark et al.'s approach).
- Gender and household-income effects mean cross-child comparisons would be confounded — another reason to keep baseline-only comparisons.

### Gaps
- No open source retrieved gives per-year (5, 6, …, 12) commission rates or SD-RT values on a single fixed task. Would need a further search (e.g., NIH Toolbox open technical reports, Fortenbaugh et al. 2015 lifespan gradCPT) to fill in.

## Q3. Is a 3-minute CPT long enough to see a vigilance decrement in children?

### Takeaway
Probably not reliably. Standard clinical CPTs run 10–30 minutes, and even in those, performance-over-time effects in ADHD are only small-to-moderate; a 3-minute task should be scored on overall omissions, d' and RT variability, not on decrement.

### Cited Findings
- Most CPT paradigms present rare targets among rapid non-targets over 10–30 min — [Huang-Pollock et al. 2012 (PMC3664643)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3664643)
- Literature estimate (meta-analysis of 47 child ADHD studies, corrected δ): overall omissions 1.34, commissions 0.98, SD-RT 0.93, mean RT 0.61 (very heterogeneous), d' ≈ 0.98; but performance-over-time (POT) effects small: POT omissions 0.54, POT commissions 0.24, POT RT 0.27, POT SD-RT 0.22 — [Huang-Pollock et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3664643)
- The small POT effects are attributed partly to stimuli where targets and non-targets are very easy to tell apart — [Huang-Pollock et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3664643)
- ADHD deficits were explained by lower perceptual sensitivity (d') and slower drift rate, with no group difference in response bias — [Huang-Pollock et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3664643)
- Task variables (CPT model, target density) and subject variables (age, IQ) strongly influence children's vigilance; the "AX/BX"-type model produced more rapid vigilance decay in children — [Univ. of Hawaii scholarspace record](https://scholarspace.manoa.hawaii.edu/items/3657dda8-1f44-4132-ad91-3285221e8bb2)
- A child auditory CPT (ages 6–11; 100 words, 20 targets) did produce a vigilance-decrement score — [PMC9443724](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9443724/) (search summary; duration not extracted)
- Reference SART design (adolescents 11–18): 540 trials, 60 no-go (1 in 9), 400 ms digit + 750 ms mask (1150 ms response window), 12 min — [Dunning et al. 2021](https://www.mrc-cbu.cam.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/www/sites/3/2021/06/Dunning-et-al.-2021.-SART.pdf)

### Inferences
- At 3 minutes with rare targets (e.g., 20% of ~150 stimuli ≈ 30 targets), a first-half vs second-half decrement would rest on ~15 targets per half — too few for a reliable within-child slope. Report decrement only as an exploratory field, not in the 0–100 score.
- If decrement matters, a lower-target-density or harder-discrimination design increases sensitivity, per the meta-analysis's discriminability point, but raises frustration risk.

### Gaps
- No retrieved study directly tested CPTs of ≤ 3 min in 5–12-year-olds for decrement detection.

## Q4. Which of the four tasks are redundant, can "distractibility" be measured separately, and is wait_for_the_bell distinct from go/no-go?

### Takeaway
stars_not_clouds (frequent-go go/no-go) and watch_the_pond (rare-target CPT) map onto two factors that separate empirically in children (response inhibition vs sustained attention) and can even be one game with two phases. wait_for_the_bell is theoretically distinct (delay aversion vs inhibition) but has weak psychometric support as a short tablet measure. Distractibility can be measured as a *within-task cost* (performance with vs without distractors), but a separate short game is unlikely to yield a reliable stand-alone score; fold it in as a distractor condition.

### Cited Findings
- A single combined GNG/CPT task yielded two separable latent factors (response inhibition, sustained attention) in 3–12-year-olds — [Clark et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10041761)
- Dual-pathway model: separate cognitive (inhibitory/executive) and motivational (delay aversion) routes to ADHD; factor analysis dissociated inhibitory deficits (go/no-go, set shifting) from delay aversion (choice delay), with delay of gratification cross-loading on both — [Solanto et al./Sonuga-Barke work, PMC2565443 and related](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2565443) (search summary)
- Delay aversion frames impulsivity as a *choice to avoid delay*, not an inability to inhibit; it appears as impulsiveness when delay can be escaped and as inattention/hyperactivity when delay is fixed — [PMC2908283](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2908283) (search summary)
- Children with ADHD respond more during imposed delay periods on the Delay Frustration Task (DeFT), indexing frustration with delay — [Chan et al. 2024, J Child Psychol Psychiatry (PMC12062859)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12062859/) (search summary)
- Irrelevant-distractor paradigm (adults): cartoon-character images on 10% of 480 letter-search trials; ADHD group showed more than double the distractor interference of controls (η²p = .231); inattentive symptoms predicted distractor cost (r² = .203); high perceptual load removed distraction equally in both groups — [Forster et al. 2013/2014, Neuropsychology (PMC3906797)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3906797)
- 7-year-olds stopped being distracted by visual letter distractors at a lower task difficulty than adults (smaller attentional capacity) — [Frontiers for Young Minds 2017, Lavie lab](https://kids.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/frym.2017.00008)
- Distraction by task-irrelevant novel sounds decreases with age across 7–10 years; novel environmental sounds impaired more than pitch deviants; children (not adults) were more distracted by novel than repeated sounds — [PMC7935912](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7935912/)
- Affective SART used task-irrelevant background sounds as distractors; adolescents performed worse in negative vs neutral sound contexts — [Dunning et al. 2021](https://www.mrc-cbu.cam.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/www/sites/3/2021/06/Dunning-et-al.-2021.-SART.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Distractibility**: a difference score (distractor trials minus clean trials) is inherently less reliable than either component, and with distractors on ~10% of trials a short game yields few distractor trials. Recommended: embed distractors (visual cartoon pop-ups, novel sounds) into watch_the_pond or stars_not_clouds on a fixed schedule and report "distractor cost" as a provisional sub-score with low confidence until pilot reliability is known. Keep `distractibility` as a dimension key only if the pilot shows the cost score has acceptable reliability; otherwise fold into `sustained_attention`.
- Perceptual load matters: in a very easy task, distractors interfere more; in a hard task, they may not interfere at all — and the "hard" threshold is lower for younger children. Age-banding difficulty therefore also changes distractor cost, which complicates comparing cost across a child's age-band transitions (treat as baseline reset).
- **wait_for_the_bell**: distinct construct in theory, but delay-of-gratification cross-loads with inhibition, and DeFT-style "responses during delay" counts are low-frequency events. Keep only if short and cheap, and score it as a provisional contributor to `impulse_control`, not a separate dimension.
- Novelty of distractors decays (children habituate to repeated distractors), so a fixed distractor set reused across days will show declining cost — a practice confound for the day-1 vs day-7 comparison.

### Gaps
- No reliability (test-retest or split-half) for a distractor-cost score in 5–12-year-olds found.
- No reliability data found for DeFT or other waiting tasks in school-age children.

## Q5. ADHD and autism performance patterns, confounds and engagement

### Takeaway
RT variability is the most robust ADHD-related signal (g ≈ 0.76 vs typical peers) but is not specific (only g ≈ 0.25 vs other clinical groups) and is reduced by stimulants. Autism shows relatively intact inhibition accuracy but elevated RT variability on go/no-go. Both points argue for treating RT variability as a skill signal, never a marker.

### Cited Findings
- Meta-analysis of 319 studies: children/adolescents with ADHD show greater RT variability than typically developing peers (Hedges' g = 0.76) but only slightly more than clinical controls (g = 0.25); mean-RT slowing disappears after controlling for variability, whereas variability effects remain after controlling for mean RT; stimulants attenuate variability, non-stimulant and psychosocial treatments do not — [Kofler et al. 2013, Clinical Psychology Review](https://sciences.ucf.edu/psychology/childrenslearningclinic/wp-content/uploads/sites/24/2013/05/Kofler-et-al.-2013-RT-Variability-Meta-analysis.pdf)
- Children with ADHD show larger ex-Gaussian tau (more very slow responses / lapses); effects on mu and sigma less consistent — [PMC3807832 and related](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3807832); [Neuropsychology Review 2023 (s11065-023-09587-2)](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s11065-023-09587-2)
- CPT omissions, commissions and SD-RT all show large ADHD effects (δ ≈ 0.93–1.34) — [Huang-Pollock et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3664643)
- Autism: response inhibition relatively intact in prior work, but the ASD group had greater SD of RT on go/no-go; typically developing children showed lower intra-individual coefficient of variation than ADHD, ODD/CD and ASD groups; ASD also showed more premature responses — [IMFAR 2010 abstract](https://imfar.confex.com/imfar/2010/webprogram/Paper6503.html); [PMC6013506 table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6013506/table/Tab3) (search summaries)
- Children with ADHD show increased attention to irrelevant areas after irrelevant distractors in classroom video tasks — [KTU AVESIS record](https://avesis.ktu.edu.tr/yayin/7e715c6b-807c-40b0-b894-2fb25505167f/assessing-sustained-attention-of-children-with-adhd-in-a-class-flow-video-task) (search summary)

### Inferences
- Score RT variability with ex-Gaussian tau or a robust SD/CV on correct go trials; require enough go RTs (dozens, not a handful) before giving it meaningful confidence.
- Medication status on test day can move RT variability; log a parent-reported "medication today" context field only if consent/scope allows, otherwise expect noise.
- Premature responses in autism and delay-related responding in ADHD both inflate wait_for_the_bell "early tap" counts for different reasons — another reason not to treat it as a clean single construct.

### Gaps
- No direct evidence retrieved on engagement/acceptability of gamified attention tasks specifically in autistic or ADHD 5–12-year-olds (e.g., sensory load of sounds, preference for predictability). Should be checked in a dedicated search and in Phase 0 interviews.

## Q6. Pakistan / Urdu localisation

### Takeaway
A Pakistani adaptation of a tablet EF battery (including a go/no-go task) was feasible with Urdu audio instructions, with 79–100% task completion, no DIF across income groups, and a notable ceiling on the go/no-go task. Use culturally neutral, readable-free stimuli and spoken instructions.

### Cited Findings
- EF Touch adapted for Pakistani preschoolers (3.1–5.99 y, n=120): forward-backward Urdu translation (Brislin), expert cultural review; instructions in Urdu — [Toor & Hanif 2022, PLoS ONE (PMC9477268)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9477268/)
- Experts recommended replacing the "pig" stimulus of the go/no-go task for cultural/religious reasons (testing showed no measurable impact) — [Toor & Hanif 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9477268/)
- Literature estimate: task completion 79.2%–100%; go/no-go ("Pig", 40 items) ceiling 21.7%; no floor effects; no DIF across low/middle-income groups; >80% administrations rated good quality; all children had family touchscreen access — [Toor & Hanif 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9477268/)
- Tablet tools with minimal/no language demands are easier to adapt; images may need adjusting even within one country; lack of prior smart-device exposure may not reduce measurement accuracy — [McHenry et al. 2023 (PMC9949664)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9949664/)
- Pakistan's multilingual environment (regional languages, Urdu, English) creates distinct demands — [Pakistan J. Psychological Research article](https://pjpr.scione.com/cms/fulltext.php?id=1096) (search summary)

### Inferences
- Stars/clouds, pond creatures, bells and garden imagery are culturally neutral; avoid pigs/dogs as targets, and avoid religiously loaded imagery.
- A 21.7% ceiling on a 40-item go/no-go even in preschoolers suggests short, slow go/no-go versions will hit ceiling in 8–12-year-olds — reinforcing faster timing / lower no-go predictability for older bands.
- Instructions should be spoken (Urdu/English per `home_languages`) with animated demonstration plus practice trials, since 5–7-year-olds may not read.

### Gaps
- No Pakistani data found for school-age (5–12) children on these paradigms; no Urdu-specific findings on auditory distractors or bell sounds.

## Q7. Per-task design notes and verdicts

### Takeaway
Keep stars_not_clouds (core impulse_control) and watch_the_pond (core sustained_attention); redesign distraction_garden as a distractor condition inside one of them; keep wait_for_the_bell only as a short, provisional impulse_control contributor (or drop if week-1 time is tight).

### Cited Findings
- **stars_not_clouds** — maps to go/no-go with frequent go (25% no-go in Clark et al.; SART uses ~11% no-go). Literature estimates for timing by age: 5–6 y 750 ms stim / 750 ms ISI; 7+ y 500 ms / 500 ms; 80–100 trials per phase — [Clark et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10041761); SART 1-in-9 no-go, 400 ms + 750 ms — [Dunning et al. 2021](https://www.mrc-cbu.cam.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/www/sites/3/2021/06/Dunning-et-al.-2021.-SART.pdf). Best metrics: commission rate, d', plus RT variability on go trials — [Clark et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10041761); [Kofler et al. 2013](https://sciences.ucf.edu/psychology/childrenslearningclinic/wp-content/uploads/sites/24/2013/05/Kofler-et-al.-2013-RT-Variability-Meta-analysis.pdf)
- **watch_the_pond** — maps to rare-target CPT (Clark et al. CPT phase: targets 25%). Best metrics: omissions, d', SD-RT/tau; vigilance decrement weak even in long CPTs — [Huang-Pollock et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3664643)
- **wait_for_the_bell** — maps to delay/waiting (DeFT-like); construct distinct from inhibition in factor analyses but delay of gratification cross-loads — [PMC2565443](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2565443); [PMC12062859](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12062859/)
- **distraction_garden** — maps to irrelevant-distractor interference (cartoon distractors ~10% of trials) — [Forster et al. (PMC3906797)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3906797); auditory novel-sound distraction declines across 7–10 y — [PMC7935912](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7935912/)

### Inferences
- **Scoring to 0–100 + confidence (proposal, not sourced as a standard):** compute per-child d' (with a log-linear correction for 0/100% rates), commission rate, omission rate and tau/CV on correct go RTs; map each to 0–100 within the child's age-band version via fixed, pre-declared anchors (e.g., d' 0→0, d' ≥ 3→100), so the mapping is deterministic and explainable (rule #7). Confidence = f(valid no-go/target trial count, number of sessions) scaled so that a single short session in the 5–7 band caps around the ~.65 reliability Clark et al. saw for that age; raise toward ~.85 only after a repeated session. Scores from different age-band versions are not directly comparable — treat a band change as a baseline reset.
- **No fail states:** replace buzzer/broken-net feedback with neutral or effort-based feedback (e.g., the star simply passes); note this departs from the validated design, so pilot reliability must be re-checked.
- **Age bands:** 5–7 slow version (≈ 750–1500 ms stim, fewer trials, longer ISI), 8–10 and 11–12 faster (≈ 500 ms) with lower no-go probability or jittered ISI to avoid ceiling, extrapolated from Clark et al.'s versions and the EF Touch ceiling finding.
- **Verdicts:**
  - stars_not_clouds — KEEP (core `impulse_control`); add age-banded timing, ≥ 80 trials, neutral feedback.
  - watch_the_pond — KEEP (core `sustained_attention`), but do not score vigilance decrement at 3 min; consider merging with stars_not_clouds as a second phase of the same game (Clark et al. model) to save setup time.
  - wait_for_the_bell — REDESIGN or DROP: keep only as a ~1-min provisional `impulse_control`/delay contributor with low confidence; not a separate dimension.
  - distraction_garden — MERGE/REDESIGN: implement as a distractor condition within watch_the_pond or stars_not_clouds; report distractor cost as provisional until pilot reliability is known; do not keep `distractibility` as a stand-alone scored dimension unless the pilot supports it.

### Gaps
- No open-source, per-age reference values for the 0–100 anchors; these must come from the pilot.
- Whether merged two-phase games remain acceptable (length ~8 min) for 5–7-year-olds with ADHD is untested.
